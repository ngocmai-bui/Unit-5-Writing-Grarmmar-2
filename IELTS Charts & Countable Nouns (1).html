Part 1 · Theory

The language of charts

Before the exercises: what kind of chart is it, which words describe change, and how do we compare two things?

1Line graph or bar chart?

Line graph

Shows change over time. You describe a trend: it rose, fell, levelled off.

Bar chart

Shows separate categories. You compare: the highest, the lowest, twice as many. Some bar charts also show time (Exercises 3 and 5).

Mẹo

Task 1 always needs an overview: the main trend or the biggest difference, with no exact numbers. Start it with Overall, …

Part 2 · Theory

Countable and uncountable nouns

Chart descriptions are full of quantities. Countable or uncountable decides which words go next to the noun.

ATwo kinds of noun

Countable [C]

Has a singular and a plural. You can put a number in front.

a house · two houses · 270 houses

a visitor · 150,000 visitors

Uncountable [U]

Only one form. No plural, no number, no a/an.

water · pollution · information

two informations → two pieces of information

Mẹo

Một số từ trong tiếng Việt đếm được nhưng tiếng Anh thì KHÔNG: advice, information, news, research, homework, furniture. Never add -s to them.

BUsing a / an

Singular countable: never alone

It needs a/an, the, my, this…

Newtown has a school. Newtown has school.

It's an interesting chart.

an before a vowel sound: an hour, an umbrella, a university.

Plural and uncountable: can be alone

Use them alone for things in general.

Houses are expensive. (houses in general)

Traffic causes stress.

Need "one" of an uncountable? Use a … of:

a piece of advice · a bowl of rice · a litre of water

IELTS

Chart sentences often start with The number of …. The verb agrees with number, so it is singular: The number of visitors was 150,000.

DNouns that trick IELTS candidates

These are uncountable in English. Learn them as a group:

newsmoneyfurnitureaccommodationluggagehomeworktransportevidenceprogressweather

These are countable and need a/an or -s:

an ideaa problema journeyan opiniona vehiclea surveyan exama reason

ESmall words, big mistakes

there · their · they'reThere are zebras. Their numbers rose. They're (= they are) safe.
than · thenHigher than last year (comparing). It rose, then fell (after that).
to · tooIt rose to 80. The ocean is too crowded (= more than OK).
the · theyThey have the same diet. (they = a subject pronoun)
